8 quick guides

SHAPE

Taper vs fade

Taper: shortening stays mainly at the temples, sideburns and neckline, leaving most of the side full.

Fade: the short-to-long transition extends farther across the sides and back.

See the visual comparison →

PLACEMENT

Low, mid and high

Low transitions sit closer to the ears and neckline. Mid transitions move toward the middle of the sides. High transitions start much higher.

The fade height describes placement. Skin or shadow describes the shortest finish; these are separate choices.

Compare fade heights →

LENGTH

Common guard references

#0.5 ≈ 1.5 mm · #1 ≈ 3 mm · #1.5 ≈ 4.5 mm · #2 ≈ 6 mm · #3 ≈ 10 mm · #4 ≈ 13 mm.

These are approximate values, not universal specifications. Guard lengths and blade settings vary by clipper system.

TOOL CONTROL

Clipper lever positions

On many adjustable clippers, closed leaves a shorter blade setting and open leaves a little more length. The amount depends on the clipper.

Check the maker’s guide. Practice lever changes on a swatch and change only one setting at a time.

Practice lever control →

LENGTH FLOW

Short → transition → longer

A fade gradually connects a shorter area to longer hair. The chosen cut and the actual head determine where those zones sit.

Use zones to read the shape, not as a fixed cutting formula.

Study the beginner cutting map →

HEAD MAP

Landmarks to look for

Temple: where the front hairline turns to the side. Parietal ridge: where the side starts curving toward the top.

Occipital area: the curve at the back above the nape. Crown: check the natural swirl and growth direction.

Open the head shape explorer →

MOVEMENT

Use a controlled pass

Keep a relaxed grip and light pressure. Follow the head’s curve, then lift away gradually as the tool leaves the hair.

Brush loose hair away and inspect before changing a guard or repeating a pass.

Practice a flick-out motion →

SAFE PRACTICE

Keep the practice controlled

Start with tools off when learning a grip or path. Practice cutting movements on a mannequin and protect the ears, neck and skin.

Clean and disinfect tools as their makers and product labels direct. Seek experienced supervision for skin-close work.
